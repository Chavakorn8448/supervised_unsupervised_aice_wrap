2 · Classification with K-Nearest Neighbors

A classifier sorts things into categories. KNN is the friendliest one: to label a new point, it just looks at its k closest neighbors and takes a vote.

Controls

Click empty space to drop a training point. Click the ★ test point mode to place the mystery point KNN must classify.

Drop some red & blue points, then place a ★ test point.

Notice how changing k can flip the prediction. Small k = sensitive to noise; large k = smoother but can ignore local detail.

🎯 Try this: build two clear groups, place the ★ right between them near the smaller group, then drag k from 1 → 15 and watch the vote flip.

3 · Regression: predicting a number

Classification predicts a category. Regression predicts a number — like price from size, or score from hours studied. We fit the line that's as close as possible to all the points.

Controls

Click to add data points. Drag points to move them and watch the best-fit line chase them.

Line: y = …
Error (MSE): —

The computer finds the line with the smallest total error. That's literally all "training" means here.

🎯 Try this: add one point far away from the rest (an outlier) and watch how much it drags the whole line. Real data is messy!

4 · Clustering with K-Means

Now there are no labels — this is unsupervised learning. The machine must discover groups on its own. K-Means finds k cluster centers and pulls points toward the nearest one.

Controls

Press Step to: (1) color each point by nearest center, then (2) move each center to its group's average.

Each step has two moves: assign points to the closest center, then update centers to the middle of their points. Repeat until nothing moves.

🎯 Try this: when there are clearly 4 blobs, set k = 2 and Auto-run — see how the machine is forced to lump groups together. Then raise k to match.

5 · Decision Trees, Random Forests & Grid Search

A decision tree asks yes/no questions to split the data into boxes. A random forest is a crowd of trees voting together. Grid search is how we hunt for the best settings.

Decision boundaries: one tree vs. a forest

Controls

1 tree = sharp, blocky boundaries that can overfit. More trees = smoother, more reliable.

🎯 Try this: set 1 tree at depth 8 — see the jagged, over-fitted boxes. Now drag trees up to 40 and watch the boundary smooth out. That's the power of a forest.

Grid Search: finding the best settings

Models have knobs (hyperparameters). Grid search just tries every combination and keeps the best. Hover a cell to see its score — the 🏆 is the winner.

6 · Artificial Neural Networks

A neural network is built from tiny units called neurons. Each neuron multiplies its inputs by weights, adds them up, and fires a signal. Stack enough of them and the network can learn almost anything.

Meet one neuron

Slide the weights and watch the neuron's output change. The activation function squashes the result between 0 and 1 — "how strongly should I fire?"
